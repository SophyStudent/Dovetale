 So how do we go about creating them for the different purposes
I suggest we create them one at a time, in the right order, so that all three documents communicate the same service but serve different purposes.

We shouldn't simply take the ten pillars and copy them into three different documents. We need to decide what each document should communicate, how much detail it should contain, and what action we want the reader to take.

Here's how I would approach it.

Step 1. Establish your core service identity
Before designing any of the documents, we need to establish the central message that connects them.

This includes:

Service name: What do you call this overall service?

Service description: What exactly do you help businesses build?

Business problem: What problems does your service solve?

Value proposition: Why should a business invest in this instead of simply hiring someone to run ads or manage social media?

Target clients: What types and sizes of businesses are you serving?

Your approach: How do you assess, design, implement and improve their marketing infrastructure?

This becomes the foundation for all three documents.

Step 4. Our actual creation process
I recommend we work in this sequence:

Establish your service identity and positioning.

Develop the complete content for the brochure.

Refine the content and turn it into a professionally designed brochure.

Develop the presentation using the same service identity, but with more explanation.

Create a reusable proposal template that can be customized for each client.

One important thing: the ten pillars remain your complete internal implementation framework. The client-facing materials will explain the value and scope of those pillars without overwhelming clients with every technical task.

Let's begin with the foundation.
I would rather establish exactly how your service should be positioned before writing the brochure. Otherwise, we risk creating beautiful documents that don't communicate the uniqueness of what you're offering.

My first question: Do you already have a name for this service, or should we develop the service name and positioning together?

What of for the website?
The website should actually be one of the most important places where your client-facing service information lives.

In fact, I would treat it as a fourth client-facing asset, not just another place to upload your brochure.

Unlike the brochure, which is a document you send to someone, your website is your permanent online business presence. It should explain your service, establish credibility, attract potential clients and generate enquiries.
