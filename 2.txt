Pillar 2: Audience Intelligence & Segmentation
The purpose of this pillar is to understand who the business should be targeting, why those people would buy, what influences their decisions, and how to group them into meaningful audience segments.

Pillar 1 established what the business offers, how it operates, how it has performed, and where its market opportunities lie.

Pillar 2 takes those findings and investigates the people the business needs to reach. The goal is not simply to identify a target age group or gender. It is to develop a detailed understanding of potential customers, existing customers, their behaviors, motivations, needs, buying intentions, and decision-making processes.

This intelligence will guide content creation, advertising, SEO, landing pages, lead generation, email marketing, and customer retention in the subsequent pillars.
1. Audience research and data collection
First, gather as much information as possible about the business's existing and potential customers.

Tasks and activities
- Identify all available sources of customer and audience information:
  - CRM records.
  - Customer databases.
  - Purchase and transaction records.
  - Lead generation forms.
  - Website analytics and GA4.
  - Meta and other advertising platform insights.
  - Social media audience insights.
  - Email marketing reports.
  - Customer reviews and testimonials.
  - Customer support conversations and inquiries.
  - Surveys, interviews, and feedback forms.
- Extract available customer information from these sources.
- Review customer inquiries to identify frequently asked questions.
- Analyze customer complaints, objections, and reasons for not purchasing.
- Examine existing customers' purchasing patterns and repeat-purchase behavior.
- Identify the products or services different customers purchase.
- Examine which marketing channels bring in the most valuable customers, where the data permits.
- Identify differences between existing customers and people who engage with the business but never purchase.
- Conduct customer interviews or surveys where existing data is insufficient.
- Research potential customers beyond the business's current customer base.
- Identify information gaps that require additional research.
Key deliverables
- Audience research report.
- Customer data inventory.
- Existing customer insights report.
- Customer feedback and objection analysis.
- Audience research gap assessment.
- Customer behavior observations.
2. Demographic and geographic segmentation
This involves identifying the measurable characteristics of the people who make up the business's audience.

Tasks and activities
- Identify relevant age groups.
- Analyze gender distribution where relevant and lawfully available.
- Identify geographic locations:
  - Countries.
  - States and regions.
  - Cities.
  - Neighborhoods or service areas, where relevant.
- Identify language preferences.
- Analyze income levels or purchasing power where reliable information is available.
- Identify occupation and employment categories.
- Identify education levels where relevant.
- Examine household and family characteristics where relevant to the product or service.
- Identify business characteristics for B2B customers:
  - Industry.
  - Company size.
  - Business location.
  - Revenue category.
  - Job roles and decision-making positions.
- Identify which demographic and geographic groups are most relevant to the business.
- Compare the demographic profiles of existing customers, leads, and potential customers.
Key deliverables
- Demographic segmentation report.
- Geographic segmentation report.
- Customer demographic profiles.
- B2B customer classification, where applicable.
- Priority geographic market assessment.
Important: Do not assume that age, gender, or income automatically determines buying behavior. These characteristics are useful only when they help explain meaningful differences between customer groups.
3. Psychographic segmentation
This examines the psychological and lifestyle factors that influence customer decisions.

Tasks and activities
- Identify customers' interests and hobbies.
- Identify lifestyle characteristics.
- Understand their values and personal priorities.
- Identify their aspirations and desired outcomes.
- Investigate their attitudes toward the product or service category.
- Identify their preferences and dislikes.
- Understand their motivations for purchasing.
- Identify their fears, frustrations, and concerns.
- Understand what they consider valuable when choosing a product or service.
- Identify factors that build or reduce trust.
- Examine their attitudes toward pricing, quality, convenience, status, and other relevant purchase considerations.
- Identify emotional and practical reasons behind purchasing decisions.
- Group customers with similar motivations and attitudes.
Key deliverables
- Psychographic segmentation report.
- Customer motivation analysis.
- Customer values and interests profile.
- Customer pain-point and desire analysis.
- Purchase influence assessment.
4. Behavioral segmentation
This pillar examines what customers actually do, rather than relying only on what they say or what demographic group they belong to.

Tasks and activities
- Analyze customers' browsing and website interaction patterns.
- Identify frequently visited website pages.
- Identify products or services that attract the most attention.
- Analyze content engagement patterns.
- Identify the types of content customers consume and interact with.
- Analyze advertising engagement and conversion behavior.
- Identify common paths from first interaction to purchase.
- Examine repeat-purchase behavior.
- Identify purchase frequency.
- Identify average order value where applicable.
- Identify customers who purchase frequently versus occasionally.
- Identify customers who have stopped purchasing.
- Analyze cart abandonment and checkout abandonment where tracking exists.
- Identify customers who request information but do not proceed to purchase.
- Identify customers who respond to promotions or discounts.
- Examine customer loyalty and referral behavior.
- Identify patterns in the channels and devices customers use.
- Identify seasonal or time-based purchasing patterns.
Key deliverables
- Customer behavioral analysis.
- Website and content behavior report.
- Purchase behavior report.
- Customer engagement analysis.
- Repeat-purchase and loyalty assessment.
- Behavioral segmentation framework.
5. Customer needs, pain points, desires, and buying motivations
This is where you move beyond describing customers and begin understanding the problems they want solved.

Tasks and activities
- Identify the problems customers are trying to solve.
- Identify their unmet needs.
- Identify their frustrations with existing solutions.
- Identify the outcomes they want to achieve.
- Identify what they consider an ideal solution.
- Understand the circumstances that trigger their search for a product or service.
- Identify the consequences of not solving their problems.
- Identify the practical benefits they expect.
- Identify the emotional benefits they expect.
- Understand their expectations concerning quality, price, speed, convenience, and service.
- Identify the objections that prevent them from buying.
- Identify concerns about risk, trust, reliability, and credibility.
- Identify what customers compare before making a decision.
- Determine what makes them choose one provider over another.
- Identify what would make them switch from an existing provider.
- Map customer needs against the business's available products or services.
- Identify unmet needs that could represent new market opportunities.
Key deliverables
- Customer needs analysis.
- Pain-point and frustration matrix.
- Customer desires and expected outcomes report.
- Buying motivation analysis.
- Purchase objection and barrier report.
- Customer needs-to-product mapping.
6. Buying intent and customer journey segmentation
Not everyone in an audience is ready to purchase. Some people are just discovering a problem, others are researching solutions, and some are ready to buy.

You need to identify these differences because they determine the kind of content, advertising, and follow-up each audience should receive.

Tasks and activities
- Identify the different stages customers pass through before purchasing.
- Segment audiences according to their level of awareness:
  - Unaware of the problem.
  - Problem-aware.
  - Solution-aware.
  - Product-aware.
  - Most-aware and ready to purchase.
- Identify customers who are actively researching solutions.
- Identify customers comparing different providers or products.
- Identify high-intent prospects showing strong purchase signals.
- Identify low-intent audiences who require more education.
- Identify existing customers who may be ready for another purchase.
- Identify customers who may be interested in complementary products or upgrades.
- Map the questions and concerns customers have at each stage.
- Identify the information customers need before progressing to the next stage.
- Identify the marketing touchpoints that can influence their decisions.
- Determine the most suitable content and communication approach for each stage.
- Identify the actions that indicate progression toward a purchase.
Key deliverables
- Customer buying journey map.
- Audience awareness segmentation.
- Buying-intent classification framework.
- Customer decision-making analysis.
- Stage-specific audience communication recommendations.
7. Customer personas development
Now consolidate the research into practical customer personas.

A persona is a research-informed representation of a meaningful customer group. It helps the business understand whom it is communicating with and why.

Tasks and activities
- Review all demographic, geographic, psychographic, and behavioral research.
- Identify customer groups with shared characteristics.
- Identify the most commercially relevant audience groups.
- Separate primary audiences from secondary audiences.
- Identify customer groups with different needs, motivations, and buying behaviors.
- Develop a detailed persona for each meaningful group.
- Give each persona a descriptive name.
- Document each persona's:
  - Demographic profile.
  - Geographic location.
  - Occupation or business profile.
  - Interests and lifestyle.
  - Goals and aspirations.
  - Problems and frustrations.
  - Needs and desires.
  - Buying motivations.
  - Purchase objections.
  - Preferred communication channels.
  - Content preferences.
  - Purchasing behavior.
  - Decision-making factors.
  - Preferred solutions.
  - Awareness and buying-intent stage.
- Identify the products or services most relevant to each persona.
- Identify the messages most likely to resonate with each persona.
- Document the evidence supporting each persona.
- Distinguish validated personas from preliminary assumptions where research is limited.
Key deliverables
- Customer persona document.
- Primary and secondary audience profiles.
- Persona-to-product mapping.
- Persona-specific communication insights.
- Persona validation and evidence report.
8. Audience prioritization and commercial value assessment
Not every audience segment deserves the same marketing investment. Some groups may be easier to reach, more likely to convert, more profitable, or more valuable over time.

Tasks and activities
- Evaluate each audience segment's relevance to the business.
- Assess the strength of demand within each segment.
- Evaluate the segment's purchasing power.
- Assess purchase frequency and potential customer lifetime value where data permits.
- Evaluate the potential profitability of each segment.
- Examine acquisition costs for existing customer groups where measurable.
- Identify segments with high purchase intent.
- Identify segments with strong repeat-purchase potential.
- Assess how accessible each segment is through available marketing channels.
- Identify segments that are expensive or difficult to acquire.
- Compare potential commercial value across segments.
- Rank segments according to business objectives and available evidence.
- Recommend which segments should receive priority in marketing activities.
Key deliverables
- Audience opportunity assessment.
- Segment profitability and value analysis.
- Priority audience ranking.
- Primary and secondary target audience recommendations.
- Audience acquisition priority framework.
9. Audience-to-channel and targeting framework
Understanding an audience is not enough. You must translate that understanding into a practical framework for reaching them.

This section creates the connection between audience research and the content, advertising, and distribution systems that will be developed in later pillars.

Tasks and activities
- Identify the platforms and channels used by each audience segment.
- Determine where different audience groups discover information.
- Identify their preferred content formats:
  - Short-form videos.
  - Long-form videos.
  - Articles and blog posts.
  - Social media posts.
  - Infographics.
  - Case studies.
  - Email newsletters.
  - Product demonstrations.
  - Reviews and testimonials.
- Identify the types of information each audience seeks.
- Identify relevant search behaviors and search intentions.
- Identify the keywords, phrases, and questions associated with their needs.
- Identify communities, groups, forums, and online spaces where relevant audiences participate.
- Determine suitable advertising audience characteristics and targeting options on each platform.
- Identify potential first-party audience segments, such as customer lists and website visitors, subject to consent and platform requirements.
- Identify remarketing opportunities.
- Map each persona to suitable acquisition channels.
- Map audience awareness stages to suitable content types.
- Recommend the most suitable channel combinations for each priority segment.
- Identify channels that may not be worthwhile for particular audiences.
Key deliverables
- Audience-to-channel mapping.
- Platform preference analysis.
- Content preference matrix.
- Search intent and audience keyword insights.
- Advertising audience targeting framework.
- Remarketing audience recommendations.
10. Audience intelligence consolidation and strategic recommendations
The final step is to bring all the research together into a usable audience intelligence system.

Tasks and activities
- Consolidate all audience research findings.
- Document the different audience segments.
- Finalize the customer personas.
- Identify the most commercially valuable audience groups.
- Document the needs, motivations, objections, and purchase triggers of each group.
- Map each audience group to relevant products and services.
- Map each audience group to its preferred channels.
- Identify content opportunities for each audience segment.
- Identify advertising targeting opportunities.
- Identify customer acquisition and retention opportunities.
- Document unresolved research questions.
- Identify additional data that should be collected going forward.
- Establish a process for periodically reviewing and updating audience information.
- Prepare audience intelligence documentation for use by the teams or systems responsible for content, advertising, conversion, and retention.
Key deliverables
- Consolidated audience intelligence report.
- Final customer segmentation framework.
- Customer persona library.
- Priority audience strategy.
- Audience-to-product mapping.
- Audience-to-channel mapping.
- Audience messaging recommendations.
- Audience research and update guidelines.

Pillar 3: Brand, Content & Organic Visibility Infrastructure
The purpose of this pillar is to build a structured system that determines how the business presents itself, what it communicates, what content it publishes, where it publishes it, and how people discover it organically across search engines, social platforms, and AI-powered search tools.

Pillar 2 established who the business needs to reach, what those people care about, their needs, interests, motivations, and preferred channels.

Pillar 3 uses that intelligence to build the business's brand communication and organic visibility system.

This goes beyond creating social media posts. It involves developing a recognizable brand identity, a consistent messaging framework, an audience-driven content strategy, a publishing system, SEO, AEO, and AI visibility.
1. Brand identity and positioning
The first task is to establish how the business should be recognized, understood, and differentiated in its market.

Tasks and activities
- Review the business's existing brand identity and positioning.
- Assess how the business currently presents itself across its website, social media, advertising, and other communication channels.
- Identify inconsistencies in brand presentation.
- Define the brand's purpose and the value it provides.
- Define the brand's mission and vision, where appropriate.
- Establish the brand's core values.
- Identify the brand's unique selling propositions (USPs).
- Define what differentiates the business from competitors.
- Establish the brand's market positioning.
- Define the brand personality and character.
- Establish the appropriate brand voice and tone.
- Define how the brand should communicate with different audience segments.
- Develop brand messaging guidelines.
- Establish the brand's visual identity requirements:
  - Logo usage.
  - Brand colors.
  - Typography.
  - Image styles.
  - Graphic styles.
  - Iconography.
  - Layout and visual consistency.
- Establish guidelines for maintaining consistent brand presentation across platforms.
- Identify opportunities to improve brand recognition and credibility.
Key deliverables
- Brand positioning document.
- Brand identity assessment.
- Brand messaging framework.
- Unique selling proposition documentation.
- Brand voice and tone guidelines.
- Visual identity guidelines.
- Brand communication guidelines.
2. Brand messaging and communication architecture
This establishes what the business should say, how it should say it, and which messages should be communicated to different audience groups.

Tasks and activities
- Develop the brand's central communication message.
- Define the main value proposition.
- Develop supporting value propositions for individual products and services.
- Translate product and service features into customer-focused benefits.
- Develop messaging around customer problems and desired outcomes.
- Create messaging that communicates the business's expertise and credibility.
- Identify key messages for each customer persona.
- Develop different messaging approaches for different audience awareness stages.
- Establish messaging for:
  - Brand awareness.
  - Customer education.
  - Product and service promotion.
  - Trust and credibility.
  - Lead generation.
  - Purchase conversion.
  - Customer retention.
- Develop brand storytelling guidelines.
- Identify suitable proof points, such as testimonials, reviews, case studies, credentials, and demonstrations.
- Establish messaging guidelines for different platforms.
- Develop a consistent terminology and language framework.
- Identify claims that require evidence or substantiation.
- Create a messaging reference document that content creators and marketers can follow.
Key deliverables
- Brand messaging architecture.
- Core value proposition framework.
- Persona-specific messaging framework.
- Product and service messaging guides.
- Brand storytelling framework.
- Trust and credibility messaging guidelines.
- Platform-specific communication guidelines.
3. Content strategy development
This is where the business establishes a deliberate approach to the content it will create and distribute.

Every piece of content should have a purpose, an intended audience, and a desired outcome.

Tasks and activities
- Review the audience intelligence and customer personas from Pillar 2.
- Identify the questions customers ask about the business's products or services.
- Identify the problems and interests that can be addressed through content.
- Identify topics relevant to each customer persona.
- Identify topics relevant to each stage of the customer journey.
- Research competitors' content strategies.
- Identify content gaps and opportunities within the market.
- Develop content pillars (the main subject areas the brand will consistently cover).
- Establish content themes under each content pillar.
- Categorize content according to its purpose:
  - Educational content.
  - Informational content.
  - Entertaining content.
  - Promotional content.
  - Authority-building content.
  - Trust-building content.
  - Community and engagement content.
  - Conversion-focused content.
- Determine which content formats are appropriate for each topic.
- Define the objective of each content category.
- Establish content distribution priorities.
- Identify opportunities to repurpose content across multiple platforms.
- Define content performance indicators.
- Develop an overall content strategy document.
Key deliverables
- Content strategy document.
- Content pillar framework.
- Content topic and theme library.
- Audience-to-content mapping.
- Customer journey content framework.
- Content format strategy.
- Content distribution strategy.
- Content performance measurement framework.
4. Content research and topic development
Before producing content, the business needs a reliable process for discovering what its audience wants to learn, search for, and engage with.

Tasks and activities
- Conduct audience question research.
- Research common customer problems and frequently asked questions.
- Identify popular topics within the business's industry.
- Research relevant search queries and keywords.
- Analyze search intent behind relevant queries.
- Identify informational, navigational, commercial, and transactional search opportunities.
- Research trending topics relevant to the business.
- Identify industry developments and emerging discussions.
- Analyze competitors' frequently published topics.
- Identify gaps in competitors' content.
- Review relevant online communities, forums, and social discussions.
- Identify recurring misconceptions and knowledge gaps.
- Identify opportunities to create original research and useful resources.
- Build a categorized content topic database.
- Prioritize topics according to audience relevance, business relevance, search opportunity, and content objectives.
- Map topics to customer personas and buying stages.
Key deliverables
- Content research report.
- Content topic database.
- Audience question library.
- Keyword and search intent research.
- Competitor content gap analysis.
- Content opportunity and priority list.
5. Content planning and editorial calendar
This converts the content strategy into an organized publishing plan.

Tasks and activities
- Establish the frequency of content publication for each platform.
- Determine the content formats to be published.
- Allocate topics to specific publishing dates.
- Assign content topics to the relevant content pillars.
- Match each planned content item to its target audience.
- Define the objective of each publication.
- Establish content production deadlines.
- Assign content creation responsibilities.
- Plan content review and approval processes.
- Schedule seasonal and industry-specific content.
- Plan campaigns and coordinated content series.
- Identify opportunities to reuse and repurpose existing content.
- Establish a system for tracking content production progress.
- Create a centralized content calendar.
- Establish procedures for updating or removing outdated content.
Key deliverables
- Monthly content calendar.
- Editorial calendar.
- Content production schedule.
- Content assignment and approval workflow.
- Content status tracker.
- Seasonal content plan.
- Content repurposing schedule.
6. Content creation and creative direction
This covers the actual production of content and the creative standards that ensure it is consistent, relevant, and effective.

Tasks and activities
- Establish content creation guidelines for each format.
- Develop creative concepts based on audience interests and content objectives.
- Create content briefs for individual publications.
- Write social media captions and post copy.
- Develop educational articles and blog posts.
- Create website informational content.
- Write product and service descriptions.
- Develop scripts for short-form and long-form videos.
- Create graphics, infographics, and visual assets.
- Develop carousels and educational visual content.
- Produce product demonstrations and explanatory videos where appropriate.
- Develop case studies and customer success stories.
- Create content using relevant customer questions and feedback.
- Establish visual storytelling standards.
- Ensure content reflects the brand's voice and messaging.
- Ensure content is accurate and supported by reliable information.
- Apply appropriate calls to action (CTAs).
- Optimize content for the intended platform and audience.
- Establish content quality-control and approval procedures.
- Develop guidelines for using AI in content research and production without sacrificing accuracy, originality, or brand consistency.
Key deliverables
- Content production guidelines.
- Content briefs.
- Social media content assets.
- Blog and article content.
- Video scripts.
- Graphic and visual assets.
- Product and service content.
- Case studies and educational resources.
- Content quality-control guidelines.
7. Social media organic visibility and management
This section establishes how the business uses social platforms to build awareness, credibility, audience engagement, and organic reach.

Tasks and activities
- Identify the social platforms relevant to each audience segment.
- Audit existing social media accounts.
- Optimize social media profiles.
- Ensure profile names, descriptions, images, and links are consistent.
- Establish platform-specific content strategies.
- Configure relevant profile categories and business information.
- Develop platform-specific publishing guidelines.
- Research relevant hashtags and platform keywords.
- Optimize captions and descriptions for platform discovery.
- Apply appropriate content formats for each platform.
- Establish a consistent publishing schedule.
- Configure content scheduling and publishing tools.
- Set up a centralized social media management workflow.
- Establish procedures for responding to comments and relevant audience interactions.
- Identify opportunities for community engagement.
- Monitor organic reach, impressions, engagement, profile visits, and audience growth.
- Identify content formats and topics that perform best.
- Review platform algorithm changes and relevant publishing recommendations.
- Identify opportunities to collaborate with relevant creators, communities, or industry accounts.
- Repurpose suitable content across platforms without simply duplicating unsuitable formats.
- Establish a social media performance review process.
Key deliverables
- Social media audit.
- Optimized social media profiles.
- Platform-specific organic content strategies.
- Social media publishing guidelines.
- Social media management and scheduling workflow.
- Community engagement guidelines.
- Organic social performance report.
8. Search Engine Optimization (SEO)
SEO ensures that the business's website and content can be discovered through relevant search engine results.

This section focuses on organic search visibility, not paid search advertising.

Tasks and activities
- Conduct a website SEO audit.
- Review the website's current organic search performance.
- Research relevant industry and customer search keywords.
- Identify high-value keywords based on relevance and search intent.
- Group keywords into topic clusters.
- Map keywords to relevant website pages.
- Identify missing pages and content opportunities.
- Optimize page titles and meta descriptions.
- Optimize heading structures.
- Improve content relevance and search intent alignment.
- Optimize image filenames and alternative text.
- Establish appropriate internal linking.
- Identify opportunities for authoritative external links.
- Review URL structures.
- Assess website indexability.
- Review XML sitemap configuration.
- Review robots.txt configuration.
- Identify crawl errors and indexing problems.
- Assess canonical tag implementation.
- Review structured data and schema markup opportunities.
- Assess mobile friendliness and page experience.
- Identify page speed and Core Web Vitals issues.
- Review duplicate and thin content.
- Optimize product and service pages.
- Optimize blog and informational content.
- Identify local SEO opportunities where relevant.
- Establish ongoing keyword and ranking monitoring.
Key deliverables
- Website SEO audit.
- Keyword research report.
- Keyword-to-page mapping.
- On-page SEO optimization.
- Technical SEO recommendations and implementation.
- Internal linking framework.
- Content gap and SEO opportunity report.
- Structured data recommendations.
- Organic search performance baseline.
9. Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)
AEO focuses on making the business's information easier for answer engines to understand, extract, and present when users ask questions.

Examples include search experiences that provide direct answers instead of requiring users to visit several websites.

Tasks and activities
- Identify the questions customers ask about the business's industry, products, and services.
- Research question-based search queries.
- Identify opportunities to provide clear, direct answers.
- Develop FAQ content.
- Structure relevant pages around specific customer questions.
- Create concise answers supported by useful explanations.
- Organize informational content using descriptive headings.
- Use clear terminology and consistent entity references.
- Implement relevant FAQ, product, organization, and other applicable schema markup.
- Ensure business information is consistent across relevant online properties.
- Strengthen author and business credibility signals.
- Support important claims with credible evidence and references.
- Improve the structure and clarity of informational pages.
- Identify opportunities to appear in featured snippets and other answer-oriented search results.
- Monitor visibility for relevant question-based searches.
Key deliverables
- AEO audit.
- Customer question and answer library.
- FAQ content framework.
- Question-focused content optimization.
- Structured data implementation recommendations.
- AEO visibility monitoring framework.
10. AI search and generative engine visibility
This focuses on improving the likelihood that the business is accurately understood, referenced, and surfaced by AI-powered discovery systems.

Examples include ChatGPT search, Google AI search experiences, and other generative answer platforms.

Tasks and activities
- Assess the business's current visibility across relevant AI search experiences.
- Identify questions for which the business should ideally be discoverable.
- Test relevant industry, product, and service queries.
- Check whether AI systems accurately identify the business and its offerings.
- Identify inaccuracies, missing information, and weak brand representation.
- Review the quality and consistency of the business's information across its website and other credible sources.
- Strengthen clear descriptions of the business, products, services, and expertise.
- Publish useful, original, authoritative content that addresses customer needs.
- Establish clear author and organizational credibility information.
- Ensure relevant pages are accessible to search crawlers, subject to platform-specific crawler policies.
- Review robots.txt and other relevant technical access controls.
- Implement appropriate structured data.
- Strengthen the consistency of business details across credible third-party sources.
- Identify relevant industry publications, directories, and authoritative websites where the business could earn legitimate mentions.
- Develop a process for monitoring AI search visibility and accuracy.
- Track changes in brand mentions and citations where the platforms expose this information.
Key deliverables
- AI visibility audit.
- AI search query testing report.
- Brand representation and accuracy assessment.
- AI search content optimization recommendations.
- Entity and business information consistency report.
- AI visibility monitoring framework.
Important: No SEO, AEO, or AI visibility strategy can guarantee that a business will be cited or recommended by a particular search engine or AI platform. The objective is to improve discoverability, clarity, authority, and accessibility.
11. Content distribution and publishing infrastructure
Creating content is only one part of the process. The business also needs a reliable system for distributing it consistently.

Tasks and activities
- Identify all channels through which content will be distributed.
- Establish platform-specific publishing requirements.
- Identify suitable social media scheduling and management tools.
- Configure relevant social media accounts within the selected tools.
- Connect supported publishing channels.
- Configure content scheduling.
- Establish a centralized publishing calendar.
- Create a standardized content upload and approval process.
- Establish procedures for publishing blog posts and website content.
- Develop content repurposing workflows.
- Configure relevant integrations between content management and publishing tools.
- Establish procedures for checking published content.
- Monitor failed, delayed, or incorrectly formatted publications.
- Establish a content distribution record.
- Document publishing responsibilities and access permissions.
Key deliverables
- Content distribution infrastructure.
- Configured publishing and scheduling tools.
- Centralized publishing calendar.
- Content approval workflow.
- Content distribution documentation.
- Publishing quality-control checklist.
12. Content performance analysis and optimization
The final section ensures that content is continuously evaluated and improved rather than published without understanding its results.

Tasks and activities
- Establish performance indicators for each content category.
- Monitor organic reach and impressions.
- Monitor engagement and interaction rates.
- Track profile visits and audience growth.
- Track website visits generated by organic content.
- Monitor organic search impressions and clicks.
- Track keyword rankings where appropriate.
- Analyze content engagement by format and topic.
- Identify content that generates meaningful website traffic.
- Identify content that generates leads or contributes to sales where attribution permits.
- Compare performance across platforms.
- Identify high-performing and underperforming topics.
- Evaluate content effectiveness against its original objective.
- Identify content that should be updated, repurposed, expanded, or discontinued.
- Review SEO and AEO performance.
- Monitor AI search visibility where measurable.
- Document performance insights.
- Use findings to update the content strategy and editorial calendar.
Key deliverables

- Organic social performance report.
- Organic search performance report.
- Content effectiveness assessment.

- Content optimization recommendations.

Pillar 4: Digital Presence & Website Infrastructure
The purpose of this pillar is to establish, configure, and maintain the business's digital properties so that customers can easily find the business, access accurate information, explore its offerings, and interact with it.

Pillar 3 established the brand identity, messaging, content strategy, and organic visibility framework.

Pillar 4 focuses on the actual digital platforms and technical infrastructure through which that brand is presented to the world.

This is not simply about designing a website. It involves auditing existing digital properties, establishing the website's architecture, configuring domains and hosting, setting up website platforms, implementing essential functionality, integrating relevant tools, and ensuring that the entire digital presence works properly across devices.
1. Digital presence audit and assessment
Before building or changing anything, assess the business's existing digital properties and identify what needs to be created, improved, connected, or removed.

Tasks and activities
- Identify all existing digital properties owned or used by the business:
  - Main website.
  - E-commerce store.
  - Landing pages.
  - Blog.
  - Mobile applications.
  - Social media accounts.
  - Business directories and listings.
  - Customer portals.
  - Online booking or appointment systems.
- Collect access to the existing platforms and administrative accounts.
- Identify who owns each digital property.
- Review the current website's structure and functionality.
- Assess the visual presentation and consistency of digital properties.
- Review website navigation and information architecture.
- Check whether business information is accurate and consistent.
- Assess mobile and desktop experiences.
- Review website loading performance.
- Identify broken pages, links, buttons, and forms.
- Assess existing security configurations.
- Review the current website's SEO and technical setup.
- Identify existing integrations and connected services.
- Assess the functionality of existing e-commerce, booking, or inquiry systems.
- Identify outdated pages, duplicated content, and unnecessary plugins.
- Document technical limitations and infrastructure gaps.
- Determine which existing properties should be retained, redesigned, migrated, or replaced.
Key deliverables
- Digital presence audit report.
- Website assessment report.
- Digital property inventory.
- Existing infrastructure and integration assessment.
- Technical and functional gap analysis.
- Digital infrastructure improvement recommendations.
2. Domain, hosting and website platform setup
This establishes the technical foundation on which the business's website operates.

Tasks and activities
- Determine the appropriate domain name for the business.
- Check domain availability and ownership.
- Register the domain or verify existing registration.
- Configure domain registrar access and ownership records.
- Select an appropriate hosting provider where self-hosting is required.
- Evaluate hosting based on:
  - Reliability.
  - Performance.
  - Security.
  - Storage.
  - Bandwidth.
  - Scalability.
  - Support.
- Configure hosting and server environments where applicable.
- Connect the domain to the hosting platform.
- Configure DNS records.
- Configure SSL certificates and HTTPS.
- Establish domain redirects where necessary.
- Configure the preferred domain version (www or non-www).
- Set up business email domain records where required.
- Configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC for domain email authentication where supported.
- Select the appropriate website platform:
  - Shopify.
  - WordPress.
  - Webflow.
  - Wix.
  - Custom-built platforms.
  - Other suitable website solutions.
- Configure the website platform.
- Establish administrator and user access permissions.
- Configure the necessary platform settings.
- Set up staging or development environments where available.
- Establish website backup procedures.
- Document domain, hosting, and platform configurations.
Key deliverables
- Registered or verified business domain.
- Configured hosting infrastructure where applicable.
- Domain and DNS configuration.
- SSL/HTTPS configuration.
- Selected and configured website platform.
- Administrator access structure.
- Website backup and recovery procedures.
- Technical setup documentation.
Important: Shopify and some other hosted website platforms manage much of the hosting and server infrastructure for you. You should configure what the platform supports rather than unnecessarily recreating infrastructure that it already manages.

Pillar 5: Conversion Rate & Landing Page Infrastructure

Pillar 7: Lead Management & Sales Enablement
Purpose: Build a structured system for receiving, organizing, qualifying, following up with, and converting potential customers into paying customers.

Pillar 6 focuses on bringing potential customers into the business through advertising and other acquisition channels. Pillar 7 ensures those potential customers are not ignored, poorly managed, or lost because the business has no proper sales process.

The objective is to make sure every lead has a record, a clear status, an assigned next action, and a defined path towards conversion.

This pillar is not about taking over the client's sales department. It is about designing, configuring, documenting, and improving the systems and processes that enable the business to manage leads and sell more effectively.

Pillar 8: Email Marketing, Retention & Customer Experience
Purpose: Build a structured system for maintaining customer relationships, communicating with customers throughout their lifecycle, encouraging repeat purchases, increasing customer lifetime value, and creating positive experiences that strengthen customer loyalty.

The business should not focus exclusively on acquiring new customers. It should also have systems for keeping existing customers engaged, encouraging them to purchase again, recovering customers who have stopped buying, and turning satisfied customers into advocates.

This pillar covers the communication and relationship infrastructure that supports customers before, during, and after a purchase.

It is broader than simply setting up email campaigns. It includes customer segmentation, lifecycle communication, retention strategies, loyalty programmes, customer feedback, post-purchase experiences, and customer relationship improvement.

1. Customer retention strategy and assessment
First, understand how the business currently maintains relationships with existing customers and identify opportunities to improve repeat purchases and customer loyalty.

Tasks and activities
- Review the business's existing customer retention practices.
- Identify how the business communicates with customers after a purchase.
- Assess whether customer information and purchase history are properly recorded.
- Examine existing email, SMS, WhatsApp, and other customer communication activities.
- Analyze available historical customer and transaction data.
- Identify the percentage of customers who make repeat purchases, where data allows.
- Examine how frequently customers purchase.
- Identify products or services that encourage repeat purchases.
- Determine the average time between purchases.
- Identify customer drop-off and inactivity patterns.
- Assess existing loyalty, referral, membership, or rewards programmes.
- Identify reasons customers may stop purchasing.
- Establish retention objectives and relevant performance indicators.
- Develop a retention strategy appropriate to the business model.
Key deliverables
- Customer retention audit.
- Existing customer communication assessment.
- Customer repeat-purchase analysis.
- Customer inactivity and churn assessment, where data allows.
- Retention opportunity report.
- Customer retention strategy.
- Retention KPI framework.
2. Customer database and contact management infrastructure
The business needs an organized customer database that allows it to understand who its customers are, what they have purchased, and how to communicate with them appropriately.

Tasks and activities

  - CRM records.

  - Previous inquiries.

  - Customer name.

  - Location.
  - Purchase history.
  - Products or services purchased.
  - Purchase frequency.
  - Total customer spend, where available.
  - Last purchase date.
  - Customer status.
  - Communication preferences.
  - Marketing consent.
- Separate prospects, first-time customers, repeat customers, and inactive customers.
- Establish procedures for updating customer information.
- Configure customer database access and permissions.
- Define customer data retention and deletion procedures.
- Ensure customer information is collected and processed appropriately.
Key deliverables
- Centralized customer database.

- Customer record management procedures.
- Customer deduplication and data cleaning report.

Pillar 9: Analytics, Tracking & Attribution Infrastructure
Purpose: Build a reliable measurement system that allows a business to understand what is happening across its digital properties, marketing channels, advertising campaigns, website, sales processes, and customer journey.

The business should not simply know how much money it spends on marketing. It should be able to understand:
- Where visitors and potential customers come from.
- What they do after arriving.
- Which pages, products, and content they interact with.
- Which actions they take before becoming customers.
- Where they abandon the conversion process.
- Which campaigns and channels generate leads and sales.
- How much it costs to acquire customers.
- Which marketing activities contribute to revenue.
- What needs to be improved to increase marketing efficiency.
This pillar establishes the measurement infrastructure that makes those questions answerable.

It goes beyond installing Google Analytics or a Meta Pixel. It involves planning what should be measured, configuring the tracking systems, implementing events, connecting data sources, checking data quality, establishing attribution methods, and building useful reporting systems.

1. Analytics and measurement strategy
Before installing or configuring tracking tools, establish what the business needs to measure and why.

Tasks and activities
- Review the business's objectives across marketing, sales, and customer retention.
- Identify the business questions that analytics should answer.
- Define the key performance indicators (KPIs) for each business objective.
- Identify the digital properties and platforms that need measurement.
- Identify existing analytics and tracking tools.
- Audit existing measurement configurations.
- Determine what customer actions and business outcomes need to be tracked.
- Map measurement requirements across the customer journey:
  - Awareness.
  - Engagement.
  - Website visits.
  - Product or service interest.
  - Lead generation.
  - Lead qualification.
  - Checkout or purchase.
  - Repeat purchase.
  - Customer retention.
- Identify the data required to evaluate each stage.
- Define the reporting frequency and responsible stakeholders.
- Establish a measurement plan that connects business objectives to measurable actions.
Key deliverables
- Analytics and measurement strategy.
- Business KPI framework.
- Digital measurement requirements document.
- Existing analytics infrastructure audit.
- Measurement gap analysis.
- Measurement implementation roadmap.

An event is a recorded action taken by a visitor or customer on a digital property. The business must decide which actions are important enough to measure and how each should be recorded.

Tasks and activities

- Classify events into relevant categories:
  - Automatically collected events.
  - Enhanced measurement events.
  - Recommended events.

  - Page views.

  - Product views.
  - Service detail views.
  - Search activity.
  - Form submissions.
  - Phone number clicks.

- Identify the parameters that should accompany each event.
- Define which events are primary business outcomes and which are supporting interactions.
- Map important events to the appropriate analytics and advertising platforms.
- Establish consistent event naming conventions.
- Identify events that require additional implementation because they are not automatically collected.
- Document event dependencies and implementation requirements.

14. Attribution modelling and marketing performance measurement
Attribution is the process of assigning credit to marketing interactions that contribute to a desired outcome, such as a lead or purchase.

The objective is to understand how different channels and campaigns contribute to business results, while recognizing that no attribution model captures every customer interaction perfectly.

Tasks and activities
- Identify the attribution questions the business needs answered.
- Review the customer's typical path from first interaction to purchase.
- Identify the marketing channels involved in that journey.
- Establish an appropriate attribution approach.
- Understand and evaluate attribution models, including:
  - First-click attribution.
  - Last-click attribution.
  - Linear attribution.
  - Position-based attribution.
  - Data-driven attribution, where supported and eligible.
- Review the attribution settings available in GA4 and relevant advertising platforms.
- Establish a consistent internal reporting approach where practical.
- Distinguish between first-touch, lead-creation, and last-touch sources.
- Evaluate direct, organic, paid, referral, email, and social traffic contributions.
- Measure campaign-assisted conversions where reliable data exists.
- Compare platform-reported conversions with analytics and CRM outcomes.
- Identify the limitations of cross-device tracking, cookie restrictions, consent, and incomplete customer journeys.
- Establish a method for evaluating acquisition cost.
- Calculate cost per lead (CPL) where reliable spend and lead data exist.
- Calculate customer acquisition cost (CAC) where sales and customer data are sufficiently connected.
- Calculate return on ad spend (ROAS) where revenue attribution is sufficiently reliable.
- Evaluate customer lifetime value (CLV/LTV) where purchase history and customer identity data permit it.
- Distinguish attributed revenue from total business revenue.
- Document the limitations of the selected attribution approach.
Key deliverables
- Marketing attribution strategy.
- Attribution model assessment.
- First-touch and last-touch measurement framework.
- Channel and campaign contribution reports.
- CPL and CAC measurement framework.
- ROAS reporting framework.
- Customer lifetime value measurement framework, where data allows.
- Attribution limitations and assumptions documentation.
Important distinction: ROAS is not the same as profit or overall marketing ROI. A campaign can generate revenue while still being unprofitable after product costs, fulfillment, salaries, and other expenses. Reporting should make these distinctions clear.

Pillar 10: Marketing Automation, Integration & Optimization

The client-facing document is something you present to a potential client to introduce and explain your service.

For example, imagine a business owner encounters your service for the first time.
1. On your website: They visit your Services page and read about your offering and the ten areas of marketing infrastructure you help businesses build.
2. Through WhatsApp or email: You send them your service brochure (PDF) when they enquire about what you do.
3. During a consultation: You use a presentation to explain the service, understand their business and discuss where they need help.
4. After the consultation: You send a tailored proposal explaining the specific work you recommend, deliverables, timeline and fees.
These are related, but they are not necessarily the same document.

I would structure your client-facing materials into three main documents:
Document	Purpose	When the client sees it
Service brochure	Introduces your complete service and explains its value	First contact
Service presentation	Explains your approach and the ten pillars in more detail	Consultation or meeting
Client proposal	Specifies the work you recommend for that particular business	After initial assessment

The important distinction is that the brochure sells the service, the presentation explains the service, and the proposal sells the specific implementation plan.

You don't need to send a prospective client your entire internal implementation checklist. They need to understand what you will build for their business, why it matters and what they will get.
